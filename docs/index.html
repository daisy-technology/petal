Name your Desktops.

macOS calls them Desktop 1, Desktop 2 and Desktop 3. Petal lets you call them what they are, give each one a color, and jump between them from the menu bar.

    Double-click to name it
    
        
    Click the flower in the menu bar, then double-click a Desktop and type. The name shows in the menu bar and in large, faint letters on the Desktop's wallpaper, behind your windows.

  3. 
        3
        
    Give it a color
    
        
    Pick one of eight colors or drag along the hue strip for your own. The flower, the thin edge around the Desktop and the name on its wallpaper all take the color.
    
      
    4. 
      
  4. 
        4
        
    Click to switch
    
        
    Click a Desktop in the list and Petal takes you there. It presses your own Mission Control shortcut, so it slides over the way Control-arrow does.
    
      
    5. 
      
  5. 
        5
        
    Hold to reorder
    
        
    Press and hold a Desktop, then drag it where you want it. Mission Control keeps its own order; the list in Petal is yours.
    
      
    6. 
      
  6. 
        6
        
    Tell them apart in Mission Control
    
        
    Mission Control still labels them Desktop 1, 2 and 3, and only macOS can change that. While Mission Control is open, Petal brings each name in front of the windows, so you can see which Desktop is which even when a big app fills it.

Set it up

    
About two minutes. Petal isn't notarized by Apple yet, so the first launch takes one extra step.

    
    
      
  1. 
        
    Download and install
    
        
    Download Petal.zip, unzip it, and drag Petal into your Applications folder.
    
      
    2. 
      
  2. 
        
    Open it the first time
    
        
    Open Petal. When macOS says it can't verify the developer, click Done, then go to
    
        
    System SettingsPrivacy & SecurityOpen Anyway
    
        
    Or run this once in Terminal: xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Petal.app
    
      
    3. 
      
  3. 
        
    Name and color your Desktops
    
        
    A flower and "Desktop 1" appear in the menu bar. Click it, double-click a Desktop to rename it, and click the dot next to it to pick a color.
    
      
    4. 
      
  4. 
        
    Allow switching
    
        
    The first time you click a Desktop to switch to it, macOS asks to give Petal Accessibility access. Petal needs it to press the switch shortcut for you; naming and colors work without it.
    
        
    System SettingsPrivacy & SecurityAccessibilityPetal: on
    
        
    After you update Petal, macOS may ask again. Turn Petal off and on in that list if switching stops working.
    
      
    5. 
      
  5. 
        
    If Petal says the shortcuts are off
    
        
    Petal switches with the Mission Control shortcuts. They're on by default, but if you turned them off, turn on "Move left a space" and "Move right a space" in
    
        
    System SettingsKeyboardKeyboard ShortcutsMission Control
    
        
    Also turning on "Switch to Desktop 1" through "Desktop 9" lets Petal jump straight to a Desktop instead of sliding past the ones in between.
    
      
    6. 
      
  6. 
        
    Keep it running
    
        
    Turn on "Launch at login" in Petal's menu so your names are there every time you start your Mac.
    
      
    7. 
    

  


  

    

      
What it can't do

      
The labels inside Mission Control stay "Desktop 1, 2, 3", and Mission Control keeps its own order. The Dock owns both, and changing them means turning off System Integrity Protection. Petal won't ask you to do that. Instead it writes each name large on the Desktop's wallpaper and brings it in front of the windows while Mission Control is open.

    

    

      
Windows and Linux

      
Petal is Mac only for now. Versions for Windows and Linux are planned; watch the repository to hear when they ship.
